# Memo: Where should a UK solar and battery developer focus?

**To:** Leadership team, illustrative mid-size UK solar and battery developer
**From:** Mehak Gupta
**Date:** October 2026
**Basis:** Analysis of the Renewable Energy Planning Database, Q2 2026 extract (DESNZ, 2026)

## Answer first

The UK pipeline does not get stuck in planning. It gets stuck after consent. Development budget should follow the real constraint: secure the grid connection first, concentrate on regions where consented projects actually get built, and grow by acquiring stalled consented solar rather than by originating standalone batteries.

## What the data shows

1. **Planning approval is high and consistent.** 94% of decided solar and battery applications submitted in 2021-24 were approved (1,688 decisions), and every ranked region sits between 92% and 99%. Planning is a cost and a delay (a median of 34 weeks to decision), but it is rarely the reason a project fails.
2. **Delivery is the bottleneck.** 98 GW of solar and battery capacity holds planning consent but has not started construction. That is six times the 16 GW that is operational. Of the 653 projects consented in 2019-22, only 41% are built or under construction three to seven years later.
3. **Deliverability varies by region more than approval does.** Build-out ranges from 26% in the North East and 31% in Yorkshire and the Humber to 46% in the Eastern region. Differences among the top seven regions sit within sampling error; the robust signal is the weak bottom tier.
4. **Batteries dominate the backlog.** Batteries make up 74% of consented-but-unbuilt capacity, and battery applications reached 44 GW in 2025, five times the 2021 level.

## Recommendations

**1. Make a grid connection the first gate, not the last.** Before committing planning spend, require a credible connection date. This matches the direction of the system operator's connections reform, which cut a 722 GW queue to 283 GW with dated offers to 2035 (Knight Frank, 2025).

**2. Concentrate new solar origination in the Eastern region and East Midlands.** Both combine top-tier build-out (46%) with deep markets (12 to 16 GW of applications in 2021-24). Treat Scotland and Yorkshire as grid-first only: active but slow to build. Deprioritise the North East for greenfield work.

**3. Lead with solar and buy, rather than build, part of the pipeline.** Historically, batteries consented in 2019-22 built out slightly more often than solar (45% against 39%). The forward picture has changed: the reform left batteries about 62 GW oversupplied against need, and 153 GW of battery projects were not prioritised (Energy-Storage.news, 2025). Meanwhile 5.0 GW of solar consented by 2022 remains unbuilt. Screening those projects for connection offers could add consented capacity while skipping the planning stage.

## Risks and limits

- Build-out is measured from REPD status fields, which are compiled mainly from public sources and developers and can lag reality (DESNZ, 2026).
- Region is a coarse proxy for grid capacity; local substation headroom matters more.
- Sample sizes per region are modest (26 to 95 consents), so close rankings should not drive decisions on their own.

## Next steps with more time

Match REPD projects to connection offers to test whether grid dates explain regional build-out; add Contracts for Difference auction results to assess revenue certainty; and model project economics for the acquisition screen.
